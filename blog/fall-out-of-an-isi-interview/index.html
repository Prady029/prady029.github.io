Fall-out of an ISI Interview..

Personal reflections and lessons learned from an Indian Statistical Institute interview experience.

#interview-preparation#machine-learning#research

Not to be confused with ISI, Pakistan.

What was there to reflect upon? A personal introspection..

I had faced an interview for a research position in ISI, Kolkata recently. This is how I had prepared for that :

I think its a good way to prepare for any such positions.
  • Went through the project head’s profile and went through his publications relevant to the project.
  • Brushed up my content that I had prepared, based on the projects that I had worked in the past.
  • Opened up my Deep Learning notes and had a good read.
  • Browsed through the machine learning and data mining slides, provided by my teachers from university.
  • Also looked into Bio-informatics and Soft-computing lecture scripts, that I had prepared through-out the semesters.(Well, ISI has a stronghold over this two research areas.)
  • Finally, some statistics and DBMS, just in case they try to test the waters.

But the problem was, I did all these in last 24 hours. I am lazy, I know. Okay, now lets’ dive in to the interview part.

I will go question-wise in order of their appearance in my case.

  1. Introduce yourself.
  2. Find the output. And state your logic.
int a = 2;
if (a >> 1):
   printf("Value of a is %d",a);

3. What is supervised learning? How does loss function fit into it?

4. How do we minimize loss?

5. Does gradient descent provide convergence in general?[Yes/No]

6. How do a predictive model assign labels to an unseen example?

I tried to answer most of them. They recursively dug into my answers to check my depth. I believe this what they look for, at least for research positions.

You may get stuck at some points and its ok. I sent an answer via email just when I recalled the most appropriate answer. This was after the interview was over.

As an aspirant, it is you who needs to set the table straight. So, I put some questions to clarify their aims for the research problem. Being respectful is the key here.

Looks simple, right! But, the key takeaway is that no matter what you prepare for, basics are the gold. And for the first question, the most honest way of preparing is to seek the answer yourself.
