1a:T1b74,<p>An ever-growing to-do list from an open-source enthusiast.</p><figure><img alt="Privacy-first open-source mobile setup" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*_rxeMraOZmliHpqKysX2Xg.png" /><figcaption>@Pradyumna029</figcaption></figure><p>I have been using Google since 6th class. Remember the good old days of Orkut? That was when I was introduced to Google to create my email. That was my first step into the Pandora’s box - Internet. Since then, google has been a part of life, not only for me but every human who have ever googled anything.</p><p>Fast-forward to 2020, I have graduated with a majors in Computer Science and a specialization in Big data analytics. Going through this CS graduation, had bestowed me enough understanding about the importance of data and the impact it can have. And Google, from its inception has been the master of using data to get better and better. Google’s main business is based on ads and ads needs to be personalized. To do so, Google needs data and hence, the abundant provision of free services on its platform. This understanding had pushed me to adopt open-source alternatives. And that&#39;s how this list was created.</p><p><strong>Disclaimer:</strong><em> I have only included those steps that I have personally followed and in no way, I enforce this on anyone. You are solely responsible for your actions.</em></p><h4>1. Remove pre-loaded Google bloatwares from phones.</h4><p>Android is a familiar name in each house by now. If you have noticed, every smartphone comes pre-loaded with apps, some from Google and some from the manufacturer. These are called <em>Bloatware</em>. I suggest the following things once you get a new phone or you have backed-up every data on it &amp; ready to do the rest.</p><ul><li>Do the usual settings and reach your homepage. Then, activate “Developers’ Mode” by clicking more than 6 times(this varies with different manufactures) on the build version inside your “About Phone” section. Or simply search online.</li><li>Enable “USB Debugging”.</li><li>Now, get a computer and set-up ADB on it. I suggest the <a href="https://www.xda-developers.com/install-adb-windows-macos-linux/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">xda-developer</a> article.</li><li>Now plug your phone into the PC and click on the notification that asks you to allow the PC access to the debug module.</li><li>Now, open your cmd or terminal and type</li></ul><pre>adb device</pre><p>If everything is set up correctly, this would show you your mobile’s serial number. Now jump into the adb shell by following command.</p><pre>adb shell</pre><p>Now it would show something like this on the terminal;</p><pre>E:/ $</pre><p>We need to get the list of all google apps(actually the package name of the app) to remove them. Hence, the following command will print a list. Essentially, you can also replace google with the manufacturer’s name to even remove the company’s bloatware too.</p><pre>pm list packages | grep google</pre><p>In order to remove the apps, enter the following command and replace the quoted text with item in printed package list <em>one by one</em> without quotes and press <strong>enter</strong>.</p><pre>pm uninstall -k --user 0 &#39;package name&#39;</pre><p>The output each time would be</p><pre>E:/ $ Success</pre><p>Once, all listed packages are removed, you can now check your phone’s app gallery. It will be devoid of any bloatwares.</p><h4>2. Get the open-source alternatives.</h4><p>The tricky part here is to get the right alternatives to those removed apps.
